**Important:** This file distinguishes known facts and testimony from

## 1. Reasonable Accommodation --- Locked Refrigerator

### Request and documentary evidence

Alexander Miller sent Vince, the house manager, a text message stating:

> Hi Vince, it's Alex Miller. I have a medication which needs to be
> refrigerated. I take it once a week. Can I keep it in the locked
> refrigerator so it doesn't get stolen?

Vince responded:

> No

Miller then clarified:

> It's my diabetes medication

The screenshot of the text exchange shows the clarification as
delivered. Vince did not respond to the clarification.

### Facts developed in mock testimony

-   Miller did not initially use the words "reasonable accommodation."
-   The initial request nevertheless expressly identified a medication
    requiring refrigeration and asked for locked storage so it would not
    be stolen.
-   After Vince refused, Miller expressly clarified that the medication
    was for diabetes.
-   Vince did not respond to the clarification.
-   Vince did not ask questions about the medical need.
-   Vince did not suggest another secure storage arrangement.
-   Vince did not otherwise engage Miller in a discussion about possible
    alternatives.
-   Miller did not send another message attempting to continue the
    discussion.
-   Miller did not separately ask owner Phil Hernandez to reconsider
    Vince's refusal.
-   Miller continued to have access to the ordinary unlocked
    refrigerator.
-   Miller had previously had medication stolen from the unlocked
    refrigerator.
-   Miller did not tell Vince in this exchange that medication had
    actually been stolen before.
-   The original request did, however, expressly state that the purpose
    of locked refrigeration was to prevent the medication from being
    stolen.
-   If the medication is stolen near the beginning of a monthly supply
    period, Miller may be left without that medication for the remainder
    of the month.

### Why Miller did not escalate the request to Phil

Miller's explanation is that Melissa from StepUp, his mentor, advised
him to stop sending messages to Phil Hernandez because further messages
might cause Phil to proceed with an eviction or become more entrenched
in an eviction decision.

Melissa did not have authority over Phil or Vince and was not speaking
on their behalf.

Miller also understood Vince to be exercising managerial authority in
the house. Vince had told Miller words to the effect:

> You're only a client here, nothing more. If I want to move you out, I
> can do so in a heartbeat.

### Issue to research

Research the applicable California and federal reasonable-accommodation
law before turning the following into legal conclusions:

-   Whether the original refrigerator request, without use of the phrase
    "reasonable accommodation," was sufficient to put management on
    notice of a disability-related accommodation request.
-   The significance of Miller's follow-up expressly identifying the
    medication as diabetes medication.
-   Which party bore what obligations after management had notice of the
    disability-related need.
-   The scope and legal significance of any duty to engage in an
    interactive process in this housing context.
-   Whether a failure to engage or accommodate can constitute a defense,
    affirmative defense, or basis for other relief in the particular
    unlawful-detainer posture of this case.
-   Whether Vince's knowledge and actions are attributable to Phil
    Hernandez or another defendant.

## 2. Retaliation --- Developing Causation Theory

### Baseline relationship with Phil

Before Miller began making complaints about management:

-   His relationship with Phil was minimal but functional.
-   Miller spoke with Phil several times before moving in to make sure a
    space was available.
-   Miller texted Phil while moving in because Phil's son, who was
    supposed to pick Miller up, was very late.
-   On one occasion Phil asked Miller to pay his monthly payment a few
    days early, and Miller did so.
-   Before Miller began complaining about Vince, Phil had not criticized
    Miller's conduct, suggested that Miller move, or indicated that he
    was unhappy to have Miller in the house.

### Complaints about Vince

Miller made complaints concerning Vince's treatment of residents. The
principal complaint was that Vince treated people badly, treated
residents like children, and behaved abusively.

Examples described by Miller include:

-   Vince becoming extremely irate during a disagreement with another
    resident about laundry. Miller believed the confrontation appeared
    close to becoming physical.
-   During that confrontation, Vince threatened to kick the resident out
    and referred to the resident having been kicked out of numerous
    houses in the past.
-   Vince mocked or imitated Miller.
-   Vince told Miller that Miller was "only a client" and that Vince
    could move him out "in a heartbeat."

Relevant incidents are also described in the existing chronology and
should be tied to their underlying evidence rather than duplicated from
memory where documentary evidence exists.

### Written complaint directly to Phil

Miller has a text exchange with Phil in which Miller proposed a number
of matters that he believed should be discussed at a planned house
meeting.

Phil responded in substance that Miller was trying to change too many
things.

Miller then narrowed the matter to one principal issue: Vince was
treating everyone badly.

**Evidence to add later:** The actual text exchange should be reviewed
and quoted accurately, with its date, rather than relying on this
summary.

### Phil's APS comment --- first perceived change in attitude

Miller identifies the first indication that Phil's attitude toward him
had changed as an occasion when Phil came into Miller's room after
Miller had begun making complaints and discussing a house meeting.

Phil told Miller that he "deals with APS all the time," or words to that
effect.

At that point, according to Miller, Miller had not himself called Adult
Protective Services.

Miller interpreted Phil's statement as suggesting that APS would not
pose a problem for Phil. That is Miller's interpretation, not an
established fact. The statement itself should be preserved separately
from the inference drawn from it.

### APS meeting

At a later meeting involving Adult Protective Services, Phil repeatedly
told Miller, in substance, that if Phil were having trouble where he
lived, he would move.

Miller recalls Phil making this suggestion approximately fifteen times
during that meeting.

The exact number should not be asserted as certain unless supported by a
recording, contemporaneous notes, testimony, or other evidence.

APS worker Mr. Garcia later told Miller, in substance, that APS could
help him find another place to live. Miller perceived this as consistent
with Phil's repeated emphasis on moving.

That perception does **not**, standing alone, establish coordination
between Phil and APS.

### Phil's dealings with APS --- open factual question

Phil's statement that he "deals with APS all the time" raises factual
questions that may be appropriate for investigation or discovery:

-   How often has APS been contacted concerning Phil's houses?
-   What caused those contacts?
-   Were the contacts generated by resident complaints?
-   Which facilities were involved?
-   What issues were reported?
-   What actions, investigations, findings, or resolutions followed?
-   Is there admissible evidence showing a pattern relevant to the
    present case?

No conclusion should presently be drawn that APS was controlled by,
aligned with, or "in Phil's back pocket."

## 3. Eviction Timing and Nonpayment

### Facts presently developed

-   Miller had not previously been late with his monthly payment before
    the events at issue.
-   Before this dispute, Phil had never served Miller with a pay-or-quit
    notice.
-   On one earlier occasion, Phil asked Miller to pay a few days early,
    and Miller complied.
-   After the complaints and APS-related events, Miller received a
    pay-or-quit notice.
-   Miller presently recalls the first notice as arriving roughly one
    week after the APS meeting, but the actual chronology should be used
    to establish the date.
-   Miller acknowledges that, as far as he knows, rent/payment was
    unpaid when the notice was served.
-   The existence of unpaid rent therefore must not be ignored in
    evaluating retaliation.
-   The retaliation question is not simply whether money was owed; it is
    whether protected complaints or other protected activity were a
    substantial or legally relevant reason for management's decision or
    manner of pursuing removal.

### Notice issues to keep separate

The notice itself has potential defects and ambiguities that should be
analyzed in the appropriate notice/unlawful-detainer file rather than
allowed to obscure the retaliation analysis.

Miller recalls confusing language concerning "three days" and "fifteen
days." The exact notice controls and should be examined directly.

There is no written rental agreement. The legal significance of that
fact should be analyzed separately.

## 4. Comparator Evidence --- Currently Unknown

Miller does not presently know whether other residents were late with
their payments and, if so, whether Phil served them with pay-or-quit
notices.

This is an **unknown**, not evidence that other residents were treated
differently.

Potential discovery question:

> How did Phil and the relevant housing operation respond to comparable
> late payments by other residents?

If legally discoverable and supported by admissible evidence, comparator
evidence could help test whether Miller's treatment departed from
ordinary practice.

## 5. Other Statements Concerning Removal Authority

Miller reports that other residents made statements concerning Phil's
ability to remove residents.

In particular, Charles stated in substance that if Phil wanted someone
out, Phil could get that person gone very quickly.

This statement should not be confused with Vince's separate statement
that Vince could move Miller out "in a heartbeat."

Charles's statement, by itself, does not establish Phil's motive for
pursuing Miller's removal. Its relevance, foundation, and admissibility
need separate analysis.

## 6. Current Retaliation Sequence to Test

The presently developed circumstantial sequence is:

**Functional baseline relationship with Phil**\
→ **Miller begins raising complaints about Vince and seeks a house
meeting**\
→ **Miller sends Phil written proposed issues**\
→ **Phil says Miller is trying to change too many things**\
→ **Miller narrows the complaint to Vince treating everyone badly**\
→ **Phil comes to Miller's room and says he deals with APS all the
time**\
→ **APS becomes involved / APS meeting occurs**\
→ **Phil repeatedly suggests that Miller move**\
→ **Approximately one week later, according to present recollection, the
first pay-or-quit notice is served**

This sequence is a **theory to investigate**, not a completed finding of
retaliatory motive.

Its strength will depend heavily on exact dates, the actual text
messages, testimony, the chronology, the contents and service of the
notices, the nature of the protected complaints, and applicable law.

## 8. Vulnerabilities / Points Opposing Counsel Can Attack

These should be confronted rather than omitted:

-   Rent/payment was in fact unpaid, as far as Miller knows.
-   No direct statement has been identified in which Phil says the
    eviction is because Miller complained.
-   Miller does not presently have comparator evidence concerning other
    late-paying residents.
-   Miller's interpretation of Phil's APS comment is an inference.
-   Mr. Garcia's offer to help Miller move does not establish collusion
    with Phil.
-   Charles's statement about Phil's power to remove residents does not
    establish Phil's motive.
-   Miller did not escalate the refrigerator request to Phil.
-   Miller did not continue the refrigerator discussion after Vince
    failed to answer the diabetes clarification.
-   Miller did not tell Vince in that exchange that medication had
    actually been stolen previously.
-   The ordinary unlocked refrigerator remained available.
-   The precise legal consequences of these facts require research under
    the law applicable to this housing arrangement and the claims and
    defenses actually pleaded.

## 11. Mock-Trial Lessons

During testimony:

-   Answer the question actually asked before explaining.
-   Do not turn an inference into a fact.
-   Distinguish what Phil said from what Miller understood Phil to mean.
-   Distinguish statements made by Vince, Charles, Phil, Melissa, and
    Mr. Garcia.
-   Do not confuse Vince, the manager, with Vincent/Arthur Alvarez. They
    are different people.
-   Use exact documentary language when the document is available.
-   If the chronology contains the date, rely on it rather than
    estimating.
-   Acknowledge unfavorable facts directly, including unpaid rent and
    the lack of follow-up after the refrigerator exchange.
-   Then explain context when permitted.
-   Avoid claiming that a fact "proves" retaliation when it is one piece
    of circumstantial evidence.
